Who thought life would take such a turn?

I did not wish for it. I never imagined it. Maybe I was lucky. Maybe it was written in fate. I got a wonderful friend — not my first friend, but not like any before.

Everyone who entered my life wanted to change me. Somehow they succeeded, a little. For the first time, someone tried to change me and felt happy doing it.

The day I spent with her, she did not let me feel alone. I smiled constantly when I was with her. I do not know what would have become of me if she had not offered friendship first.

She was genuine — interested in my life, my habits, my problems. She had solutions for everything. When she said her group would make me laugh if I was sad, I did not take it seriously. I was afraid to talk to her in college at first. I am a bad conversation initiator.

The moment she chatted with me, she tracked the loneliness inside me. I had no way to hide it. The unresolved crush she addressed in a fraction of time. I felt better. I understood the real world a little more.

The more time I spent with her, the more I knew her. She had a way of making you do things. She was not outwardly attractive — anyone might misinterpret that. But once you knew her, you saw dynamism, politeness, a beauty that did not show off. She believed in inner beauty. I confessed I still fell for pretty faces until I knew someone's nature.

I got nervous around girls. She suggested I talk with a lot of girls — which sounded like a big drum banging on my ear: *a lot of exams*. The studious tag kept girls away, she said. They thought I had nothing to discuss except study. I agreed.

At home I had been taught: less talking with girls, less involvement. Maybe that was a factor. Maybe staying home, sticking to computer and books was another.

"How many girl friends do you have?"

"None."

She said I was someone every girl dreams of. I could not agree.

When I stood silently among unknown boys, she noticed and asked me to join them. That is what friends do — support when you cannot express need.

We had little in common. She was frank, open-minded, genuine. I was fake, conservative. The one thing we shared: she had experienced something I was experiencing now.

---

Then came December — and change.

Everyone wanted change in me. Attitude. Style. Expression. Thoughts. Behavior. I wanted it too. She guided me. I accepted. The change was different this time — productive, risky. I feared falling for her. I still fear it.

This friendship bond was pulling me toward something that would hurt one day. I knew that consciously.

The changed person in me became more ambitious, expected, naughty, progressive, jolly. It moved too fast. Why? Final semester? Leaving everyone soon? Unfinished tasks? Or simply because I had never had a friend like her?

She noticed the speed. I was becoming like my friends — not evil, but human. No one accepts an upside-down change in a short period. It unbalances equations with friends, classmates, teachers, family.

She made me realize where I was making mistakes.

She is the medicine to every type of disease I had.

I need a break. This changing should be controlled, progressed steadily. Maybe I misunderstood her procedure. Maybe I am possessed by her rather than by wisdom.

**This is the fear that forces me to talk with her less.**

What will happen if I get used to her?

Waiting for answers.

Addiction unsatisfied forever.

That was the price of someone who stayed — not romance, not rescue, only presence sharp enough to show me what I had been missing, and dangerous enough to make me afraid of needing it too much.
