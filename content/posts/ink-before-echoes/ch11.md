*June 2017. Four years after college ended.*

*Some mornings I wake up from a dream and cannot hold it. I reach for the image and it closes, the way water closes over a stone. Only the feeling remains — heavy, unresolved, humid. This is one of those mornings.*

*It used to be a different dream. A house, decorated with lights. Her face unclear but her presence warm. She knew my name. She knew my birthday. There was a feeling in that dream of something still possible — as if the right version of the story was just slightly out of reach but still reachable.*

*That dream I could wake from without grief.*

*This one is different. Four years have passed, and whatever was still possible in that house has found its way to a beach.*

*Here is the dream, as close as I can reconstruct it. I have written it from the outside because that is the only way I know to look at a self that far away.*

---

With a trembling heart and bent shoulders, he stands on the beach, staring at the sea. The water goes on without limit or edge. His mind keeps circling back to her — to a face that has grown blurred now, familiar in outline but uncertain in detail, the way photographs fade when you look at them too long.

The waves wash over his feet. The wet sand slips beneath him with each pull, drawing him slightly deeper. He watches the water crash against a wall of black rock and sees her — briefly, clearly — wearing an orange kurti and dark pants, her hair moving in the wind. Then the image shifts and she becomes uncertain again. He is not sure if what he is seeing is her, or only what he has assembled from remembering.

The wind blows hard. Her face sharpens in a way that feels like mercy and then like grief: darkness under her eyes, a paleness that is not quite sadness and not quite calm. No visible emotion — only a silence that feels old. She walks toward him, says something he cannot hear, and takes his hand. She looks at the ring on her finger — a heart-shaped stone — and removes it, and places it in his palm.

Then she turns and runs toward the sea.

He watches. His chest tightens. He tries to follow and finds he cannot move at all — not an inch, not in any direction. The water rises to her waist, her shoulders, her neck. He screams and hears nothing, not even himself. The waves do not stop. She does not turn back.

Then — nothing. No trace of her remains.

He realizes, too late, that the sea is taking him too. The sand is gone from under his feet. He cannot breathe. The ring in his hand heats and dissolves — small pearl-like pieces slipping through his fingers, vanishing into the dark water before he can close his fist around them.

He keeps his eyes open as long as he can.

Then he cannot.

---

*I woke up without knowing what time it was. The ceiling was familiar. The room was quiet.*

*Four years. And she still comes back in the dark, wearing orange, saying something I am never close enough to hear.*

*I have stopped trying to remember her face. What stays is not her face anyway. What stays is the feeling of the ring in my palm before it melted — the brief weight of something I was given and could not keep.*

*Maybe that is all it ever was. A brief weight. A moment of something real, offered before the tide came in.*

*I got up, made tea, and did not write about it until now.*
