_Who am I?_

That was the first question I asked myself when I landed on earth — though *landed* is too neat a word for what happened when I came out from the wonderland of my mother's body.

---

God had told me to enjoy the next sixty-eight years here, in a new environment. Not entirely unknown — in heaven we had heard something about this place. Angels took care of us there, giving whatever was needed. We were always satisfied, so there was not much to do.

I asked God who would take care of me on earth. He said, "I have created a creature — My best creation in the whole universe — called human." I asked many things about humans, but He only said, "You will understand when you experience them."

---

Before birth, I was inside a balloon-like cover — nothing more than a paralysed body, fed through a pipe connected to my host, my mother. I did not know whether I was breathing or eating, only that the surroundings were warm and slippery. Every now and then I would hit the wall and get deflected in slow fluid motion. Sometimes I heard voices that gave satisfaction; sometimes, anxiety. Someone was talking to me, but who — that was not clear.

After months of this, after several moments of fighting against something I could not name, my mother gave birth to me. I could hear her screaming, crying, fighting for the survival of both of us — but I could do nothing. A person in a white coat took me in his hands and handed me over to her.

I saw the first human creature who seemed so close to me — who had gone through suffering to hold me in her hand, who had tolerated nine months of pain to touch my little fingers, to kiss my forehead, to show her right over me.

Again the same thought came: _Who am I?_

---

Fresh air. Breathing. Smelling. Touches — some good, some I could not name. I was washed and wrapped in something called clothes. Soft, but not like the softness inside my mother's body.

Then I noticed a change in the faces around me. Something hit my back. It was not in my favour. So I reacted with a loud sound — a thing I was not yet introduced to. Later I learnt it is called crying. And then everyone's expression changed back to the earlier smile-like faces.

---

Most of the people left. Only one remained — a nurse. She showed something; my mother learnt it patiently. My mother brought me closer, laid me on her lap like a frame where I fit exactly, kept one hand on the back of my head, and fed me with something warm. Liquid. Her body's nutrients. What we call milk.

After some time I was full. The starving was no more. She lifted me and kissed every part of my face. Exhaustion was sitting on her face, but soon it changed into a satisfied, happier womanhood.

---

Then I was taken home. Many beings waited — some very tall, some a few inches taller than me. Some had beard, some did not; some had long hair, some did not. I wanted to touch them, feel them, understand them. So I pointed my finger at someone, and every time one or other person would carry me on their shoulder. Some swung me; some kept me safe. Some pinched my nose, some tickled me, some moved fingers around my stomach.

Inside, I wanted to say: Don't touch my nose every second. Don't put your finger in my mouth — it is salty. Don't swing me up; I feel lost in vacuum.

But every time they heard only one thing: my crying. There was no way those creatures could understand me. Communication problem was there from the beginning.

And still the question remained: _Who am I?_
